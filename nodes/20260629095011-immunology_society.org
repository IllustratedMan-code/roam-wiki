#+title: immunology-society


This is just a silly metaphor I came up with to explain the immune
system to people.

Imagine that the body is a civilization of people. There are many
different types of people with different jobs. There are bureaucrats,
structural engineers, cleanup crews, and those responsible for keeping
order. Imagine this society is constantly beset by zombies, aliens,
and other attackers. This society does not have any time for niceties
like respect for life. Only the continuation of the civilization is
important. Each member of this society desires to live, but is willing
to sacrifice itself for the greater good.

The immune system is equivalent to the police force, the law-keepers of
the land. Each member of this force is judge, jury, and
executioner. Even among this crew of dangerous individuals, to mount
any sort of resistance against the constant, changing threats, the
force must be coordinated.

The innate immune system can be thought of as the boots on the ground,
normal police officer that responds to everyday threats and
disputes. Macrophages act as cleanup crew and death squad, eating
everything that may pose a threat and sounding alarms for the other
members of the team.

The adaptive immune system is more like a detective agency. There are
many detectives, each with their own specialty. Some are really good
at identifying zombies, others certain species of alien, and so
on. The community of detectives is so large that there is always at
least a single individual who knows how to react to any threat you may
face. This diversity is great for adaptability, but immune responses
take time. Each detective can only hold one "wanted poster" at a time,
and for pathogens that have not been seen by the body, there may only
be a few detectives that have the required wanted poster.

For example, imagine if there was a type of zombie that only had one
defining feature that may seem innocuous. Maybe these zombies acted
normal most of the time, but grew freckles on their knees. Most people
would not think about this, but a few detectives know that these
freckles actually prove that whoever has them drinks the blood of
children in the night. When one of these detectives notices the
problem, they will work hard to distribute their wanted posters and
train other detectives to identify the freckles. This takes time, as
there are billions of detectives, and many more members of the general
cleanup crew. After this infection, there will be more detectives that
know how to identify the freckles, enough that the response takes much
less time. Some of the older grizzled detectives will stick around a
long time to make sure that the knee-freckle plague doesn't come back.

B cells and T cells are these detectives in the immune system. When
they identify cells infected with viruses, they proliferate, causing
that specific antigen receptor ("wanted poster") to be more common
among the B cell and T cell population. Some of these cells are very
long lived and act as memory cells that cause any response to
reinfection with a specific pathogen to go much faster the next time.
